## Lecture-hall text legibility prior art

Cai, H.; Kim, J.-J.; Green, P. A. (2011). *Computational Assessment of Text Legibility in Lecture Halls*. Indoor and Built Environment, 20(3), 321–332.
https://doi.org/10.1177/1420326X11400881

The paper describes a computer program using display/text geometry, lighting, and observer visual acuity to compute legible seating/viewing locations; the paper reports validation with human subjects. Therefore BACKROW does not claim to invent lecture-hall text-legibility computation.

## Projector-camera quality prior art

Le, H.; Marshall, C.; Doan, T.; Mai, L.; Liu, F. (2017). *Visual Quality Assessment for Projected Content*. CRV 2017.
https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fliu/papers/crv2017.pdf

Already includes camera-captured projection analysis and explicitly states that camera and viewer perceive projected content differently. BACKROW therefore does not claim to invent projector-camera quality assessment or the camera-human gap.

## Human text-legibility / OCR-human gap

VI-OCR — Scientific Reports 2026:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-30982-7

The paper compares OCR/VLM behavior with human recognition under visual degradation and reports that some models can fail to generalize or overperform rather than mimic human observers. BACKROW uses this to justify real human calibration/validation instead of turning OCR confidence into a human-readability percentage.

## Claim boundary

Defensible current claim:

> BACKROW is an audience-position presentation QA workflow that automatically maps a real captured projection back to its source slide, provides localized exact-information recoverability evidence plus a custom learned survivability channel, then verifies a targeted repair through rescan.

Not currently claimed:

- world's first presentation readability tool;
- world's first projector-camera quality model;
- camera equals human eye;
- measured human accuracy before locked field data;
- universal superiority to frontier VLMs before the locked same-evidence baseline.
